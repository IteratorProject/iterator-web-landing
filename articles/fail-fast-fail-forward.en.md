## The Myth of Overnight Success

Nobody wakes up successful. They wake up failing, over and over, until one day they don't.

![Cover image](iterator_cover.png)

I've collected more failures than wins in my journey. Here are the ones that taught me the most.

## Failure #1: The Perfect Project

I spent 4 months building a SaaS product. Beautiful UI. Clean code. Comprehensive tests.

**Users: 0.**

What I learned: *Perfect is the enemy of shipped.* I should have launched in week 2 with a bare-minimum version and iterated based on real feedback.

## Failure #2: The Wrong Stack

I once chose a technology stack because it was "trendy," not because it was right for the project.

- **React Native** for a simple landing page
- **Kubernetes** for a portfolio site
- **Microservices** for a todo app

I learned to match the tool to the task, not the hype.

## Failure #3: Going Solo

I tried to build everything myself. Design, backend, frontend, DevOps, marketing.

The result: burnout and a half-finished product.

**Lesson:** Find people who complement your skills. A team of average developers who collaborate well beats a genius who works alone.

## The Forward Part

Here's the thing about failure: it's only failure if you stop. Every failed project taught me something I couldn't have learned any other way.

| Failure | Lesson Learned |
|---------|---------------|
| Perfect Project | Launch early, iterate fast |
| Wrong Stack | Choose boring technology |
| Going Solo | Build with others |

Keep failing. Keep learning. Keep iterating.
